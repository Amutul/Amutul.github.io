How many agents do you need?

An intraday staffing calculator for contact centers. Enter the expected call volume and your service-level target, and it works out the staffing using the Erlang C model, the same math most workforce management tools use.

How it works

First it turns your inputs into workload, measured in Erlangs: calls multiplied by handle time, divided by the interval length. 120 calls of 5 minutes in a 30-minute interval is 20 Erlangs, meaning 20 agents would be busy every second just to keep up.

You always need more agents than the workload, or the queue grows forever. Erlang C estimates the chance a caller has to wait for each staffing level, and from that the share of calls answered within your target time. The calculator picks the smallest number of agents that meets your service level.

That number is agents on the phones. Shrinkage turns it into the number you need to schedule: if 30% of paid time goes to breaks, meetings and absence, you divide by 0.7.

Erlang C has known limits. It assumes callers never hang up and that calls arrive randomly and evenly within the interval, so it tends to slightly overstate the staff you need. Real-time teams use it as a starting point, then adjust with what they see on the floor.
